Before you start

Printer firmware

The stock U1 firmware only reads Snapmaker’s own spools. To read your own tags, install the community Snapmaker U1 Extended Firmware. Then, in its /firmware-config/ page, set Filament Detection to OpenRFID (recommended) or keep the built-in reader. Both read the tags this app writes.

Which tags to buy

  • NTAG215 or NTAG216 stickers (13.56 MHz, “NFC Forum Type 2”). 25 mm round stickers fit nicely on a spool.
  • Avoid NTAG213 (too small, and the U1 reader can’t read it), MIFARE Classic key cards, and ISO 15693 tags (the kind OpenPrintTag uses).
  • 125 kHz RFID key fobs look similar but aren’t NFC. Phones can’t see them.

Writing a tag

  1. Pick the type, manufacturer and color. Suggestions come from SpoolmanDB and your Spoolman, but you can type anything.
  2. Tap Write tag and hold the tag to your phone until it buzzes.
  3. Stick the tag on the side of the spool that faces the printer’s reader when it’s loaded. If you load spools from either side, write a second tag for the other side.

Finding your phone’s NFC spot

  • Android: usually the middle of the back, near the camera. Move the tag slowly until it’s detected.
  • iPhone: the top edge, near the camera. The system sheet shows while it’s listening.
  • Thick, metal or magnetic cases (including wallet cases and grips) block NFC.

When the printer doesn’t pick up a spool

  • Use Check to read the tag. It tells you if the data is there and if the U1 can read that chip. In the app it also asks the chip what it really is, and whether it’s locked or password protected.
  • Make sure the tag is within 1–3 cm of the reader, on the side of the spool facing the printer.
  • A vendor tag on the same spool (Bambu, Creality…) can drown out yours. Cover it with a strip of foil tape.
  • On the printer, run FILAMENT_DT_UPDATE CHANNEL=0 (0–3) to re-read a slot. OpenRFID problems show up in openrfid.log in Fluidd.
  • If the right filament shows but the wrong spool is tracked in Spoolman, the tag’s UID is linked to another spool. Check the tag here, then rewrite it and choose “Move the tag here”.

Spoolman

  • Enter the address in Settings, including the port: http://192.168.1.20:7912. Your phone must be on the same network.
  • New tags create a spool and reuse matching vendors and filaments. The tag’s UID is stored in the spool’s Card UIDs field and, on Spoolman 0.27+, as a native tag, so the U1’s Spoolman integration finds it whichever store it uses.
  • Give the Spoolman machine a fixed IP address. If it changes, both this app and the printer lose it.

What’s on the tag

One NDEF record of type application/json in the OpenSpool format: type, color, brand, subtype, temperatures, diameter and weight. Any NFC app can read it. Transparency (alpha) is left out on purpose: the U1’s two tag readers interpret it differently, and one of them rejects the whole tag over it.
